AI AmbassadorA service robot on the sales floor

Campaign · 2026.08LLM AgentASR / TTSRetailUpsell

Media coverage

當賣場開始理解人　燦坤3C攜手Sony翻轉零售體驗 PChome News · 創新聞 · July 2026

Business problem

Physical retail is running out of people who can sell. Turnover drains product knowledge from the floor, and brands' launch budgets stall at the shelf with no one to explain them.

Challenge

Floor staff cover several zones and can't master every series and spec, so new ranges rarely get a proper introduction. TVs are seldom bought in one visit, so staff keep repeating the same basics. The robot takes over the basics and frees staff for the customer care only people can give.

How I solved it

  1. 01

    Make the first move

    The robot greets passers-by and invites them closer, turning a static display into a conversation.

  2. 02

    Ask what shoppers relate to

    It asks about the room, viewing habits and who's watching, not specs. Shoppers describe needs in their own words, giving the brand demand data no POS captures.

  3. 03

    Guide, don't decide

    It compares options in plain terms, suggests premium models and matching accessories, and leaves the decision to the shopper, who takes the recommendation by text or continues with staff.

AI SommelierA wine advisor for a membership warehouse club

PoCLLM AgentASR / TTSWholesaleUpsell
Demo video · Available on request

Business problem

Wine sells on advice, but warehouse clubs run on self-service. Facing hundreds of labels and unfamiliar jargon, shoppers fear choosing wrong, so they default to the familiar bottle or buy nothing.

Challenge

Floor staff restock rather than advise, and a sommelier per store doesn't scale. One experience had to serve first-time buyers and enthusiasts alike; success meant a confident choice shoppers could explain.

How I solved it

  1. 01

    Meet shoppers at the shelf

    The robot greets shoppers in the wine aisle and lets them ask by voice or tap on screen, whichever feels easier at the shelf.

  2. 02

    Adapt to every level

    Novices are asked about taste, meal and occasion; enthusiasts can name a grape or compare two bottles. The agent meets each shopper at their level.

  3. 03

    From aisle to basket

    It narrows hundreds of labels to one bottle, suggests an optional second for the meal, adds serving tips and shelf location, and leaves the choice to the shopper.

IM SDK & SaaS PlatformThe conversation layer inside clients' products

Scaled · 2M+SDK / APIMulti-tenantB2B SaaSMonetization

Vyin Chat console · bot-to-agent handoff and channel moderation

Business problem

Real-time messaging is a feature every app wants and no one wants to maintain. Companies would rather buy it, if the platform is reliable and affordable to run.

Challenge

A mature IM product has to do two things at once: an SDK complete enough that developers can use it as-is, and a platform that prices, meters, permissions and reports on usage automatically.

How I solved it

  1. 01

    Complete SDK modules

    Public rooms for thousands, one-to-one and group chat, voice and video, plus LLM translation, summaries and moderation, each one SDK call. Together they've delivered 2M+ messages.

  2. 02

    A commercial layer

    Multi-tenancy, subscription tiers, usage metering, payments and permissions turned the SDK into a service that can be priced and run at scale.

  3. 03

    Built for operators too

    MAU/DAU reporting, an AI agent builder and an automation canvas let operators change the rules themselves, across gaming, hotel, restaurant and retail brands.
